# Tara and the Star Lantern — Series Format

**Read with:** `CHARACTER_BIBLE.md` (cast, world, signatures)
**Audience:** Preschool, 2–5 · **Made for Kids: YES**
**Runtime target:** 4–6 minutes (~30 shots at 8–10 sec)

---

## THE EPISODE SKELETON

Every episode is the same seven beats. Preschool audiences want the shape to be
predictable — the *content* changes, the shape never does. That repetition is
the product, not a limitation.

| # | Beat | Length | What happens |
|---|------|--------|--------------|
| 1 | **Morning** | ~40s | Blue Door House, morning gold. Tara, Chikoo, the lantern. World established |
| 2 | **The Task** | ~30s | Ammamma names **three things** to fetch, and gives one piece of practical knowledge |
| 3 | **Stop One** | ~50s | Easiest. Teaches the interactive rule. First lantern point lights |
| 4 | **Stop Two** | ~60s | An obstacle, not a villain. Requires a plan. Second point lights |
| 5 | **Stop Three** | ~70s | Kaaki takes something. **Clap three times.** Third point lights |
| 6 | **Home** | ~40s | The three things become one thing. Payoff |
| 7 | **The Gift** | ~30s | Kaaki returns and is given something freely. Sunset amber. Fade |

**Beat 7 is not optional.** It is the emotional engine of the whole series and
the direct sibling of Grimble's arc: *the one who takes, takes because nobody
offered.* Every episode ends by offering.

---

## THE THREE INTERACTIVE BEATS

Exactly three per episode. More than three and the child stops responding.

### 1. THE COUNT (Stop One)
Tara asks the viewer to count something on screen — mangoes, steps, birds. Always
between **one and five**. She counts along on her fingers after the pause, so a
child who guessed wrong is never left behind.

### 2. THE PLAN (Stop Two)
Tara asks the viewer *what should we do?* Ravi offers a close-but-wrong idea
first (**THE GLASSES PUSH**), then the right answer arrives. This is where the
show models thinking rather than knowing.

### 3. THE CLAP (Stop Three)
**THE FEATHER TELL** fires — Kaaki's three head-feathers stand up. Tara turns to
camera and asks for **three claps**. On the third, he drops it and flaps off.

Every one of these uses **THE CALL-OUT PAUSE**: camera turn, lantern lifted to
chest, background softens and desaturates, hold for a full three seconds. Three
seconds feels agonising in the edit and is exactly right for a three-year-old.

---

## RULES THAT NEVER CHANGE

**Always:**
- Three stops. Three lantern points. Three claps. Three of everything
- The obstacle is a situation, never a villain
- Kaaki is startled, never hurt, never chased in anger
- Tara gets something wrong at least once and says so
- Ammamma sets the task and then steps back — she never solves it
- Ends on sunset amber at the Blue Door House

**Never:**
- No jeopardy, no peril, no darkness, no chase-and-catch tension
- No character is mocked for being wrong
- No scary music sting, ever
- Nobody shouts at Kaaki
- No brand names, logos or packaging anywhere in frame
- Nothing photorealistic — see the safety position in the character bible

## THE VOICE PROBLEM — decide before Episode 1

This format **requires speech**. Scout works silently on instrumental music, but
an interactive show cannot: "How many mangoes can you see?" has no visual
equivalent. This is the one genuine production gap between the two series.

Three routes:

| Route | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Record it yourself** | Free, warm, real. Full control | You must voice a five-year-old girl convincingly, or find someone who can |
| **AI text-to-speech** | Fast, consistent across episodes, no recording setup | Must be disclosed alongside the existing AI disclosure. Child voices from TTS often land badly |
| **Near-wordless** | Keeps the current mobile-only stack | Loses most of the format's value. Not recommended |

The shot block for Episode 1 is written so the interaction reads **visually**
even with the sound off — Tara turns, lifts the lantern, holds up fingers, waits.
A separate VO script is supplied so voice can be added at any point without
regenerating a single clip. Generate the video first; decide the voice after.

## MUSIC DIRECTION — series-wide

**Important:** the music template in `FLOW_COMPLIANCE.md` §4 says to specify
*"General audience — not a baby show."* For Tara, do the **opposite**. That line
exists for Scout and must be inverted here.

- Gentle, warm, acoustic-led. **BPM 90–105**
- Instruments: soft acoustic guitar, light hand percussion, flute, marimba,
  gentle strings, a bamboo flute for the morning
- Always: *"Purely instrumental. No lyrics. No voiceover."* — the VO is a separate track
- Never: driving electronic beats, tension risers, minor-key dread, dramatic stings
- The three lantern-point moments each get a small warm chime — same chime every
  episode, so children learn to expect it
