# Tara and the Star Lantern — Character Bible

**Series:** Tara and the Star Lantern
**Format:** Interactive preschool adventure (direct address, pause-for-answer)
**Audience:** Preschool, ages 2–5 · **Made for Kids: YES**
**Relationship to Scout:** Completely separate content line. Scout stays general
audience, Made for Kids: No, "not a baby show." These two never share an episode.

---

## Why This Series Exists

There is very little English-language preschool content built around Indian
daily life. The gap is real and the teaching content writes itself — Telugu and
Hindi words, Indian counting, festivals, food, birds and animals a child in
Hyderabad actually sees. Being culturally specific is what differentiates this
from every other explorer show. It stops the comparison instead of inviting it.

---

## THE ANTI-DRIFT PRINCIPLE

Everything in this file is ordered around one hard-won rule from Scout:

> **Flow reads a character description in order and locks onto the first strong
> association it recognises.** Scout drifted to Sonic until the description led
> with braided locs and hiking boots instead of "blue hedgehog."

A young girl explorer is a *far* more dangerous prompt than a blue hedgehog,
because Flow's training data has four powerful attractors waiting:

| Attractor | Triggered by | Kills the shot because |
|---|---|---|
| The famous cartoon explorer girl | "girl explorer", "backpack", bob haircut, orange shorts | Direct IP collision — the exact thing we must never generate |
| Disney/Pixar princess | "adventurous girl", flowing long loose hair | Wrong age, wrong tone, generic |
| Photoreal child | "realistic", "real child", high detail without style anchor | **Policy violation** — see Safety, below |
| Generic "Indian girl" | leading with nationality | Renders an adult woman in bridal wear, not a five-year-old |

**Therefore every description below leads with the braids-and-ribbons and the
lantern — never with "girl", never with "explorer", never with "Indian".**
Species/role/nationality always come *after* the unique visual anchors.

## 1. TARA — the lead

**Age 5. Curious, brave, talks to you.**

### Flow Characters tab — paste this exactly

> Two long black braids hanging down past the shoulders, each tied at the end
> with a marigold-yellow ribbon. Carries a small brass star-shaped lantern with
> five points, held in one hand by a ring handle. Wears a teal-green cotton
> kurta tunic over white churidar leggings, and scuffed red canvas shoes. A
> small dark bindi dot on the forehead. Warm brown skin, large dark-brown eyes,
> round full cheeks, wide open smile with a gap in the front teeth. A young
> child of five — small, short, round-faced, clearly a little kid, NOT a
> teenager and NOT an adult. Bright, curious, confident posture; stands with
> feet planted and chin up. 3D CGI animated style, DreamWorks and Pixar
> theatrical animated film quality. Stylised cartoon proportions with a slightly
> large head — NOT photorealistic, NOT a real child, NOT live action, NOT flat
> 2D. IMPORTANT: hair is in two long braids with ribbons — NOT a short bob, NOT
> a fringe across the forehead. She carries a star lantern — NOT a backpack.

### Why this exact order

| Position | Element | What it defeats |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Two long braids with ribbons** | Kills the bob-haircut attractor before it forms |
| 2nd | **Brass star lantern** | Kills "backpack" — the other show's second-most recognisable prop |
| 3rd | **Teal kurta + white churidar** | Kills the pink-top/orange-shorts palette |
| 4th | Age and proportions | Stops the render ageing her up to 8–12 |
| 5th | Style Bible | Locks stylised CGI, blocks photoreal |
| Last | Explicit NOT list | Flow weights end-of-prompt negatives heavily |

Note what is **absent**: the words "explorer", "adventure girl", and any
nationality. Her identity comes through in action and setting, never by label.

### Visual signatures — use by name in every shot prompt

| Signature | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **THE STAR COUNT** | Each stop of the journey completed | One more point of the five-point lantern lights warm gold and stays lit — the child watching can count how far along they are |
| **THE WONDER SPARK** | Tara figures something out | 4–5 tiny gold sparks lift off the lantern and drift upward, fading at head height |
| **THE CALL-OUT PAUSE** | Tara asks the viewer a question | She turns to face camera, lifts the lantern to chest height, and the background softens and desaturates slightly for a held beat — the visual cue that it is the child's turn to answer |

**THE CALL-OUT PAUSE is the most important invention in this series.** The whole
interactive format lives or dies on whether a three-year-old understands that
they are being asked to speak. Giving that moment its own consistent visual
language — camera turn, lantern lift, world softens — teaches the child the rule
within two episodes.

### Personality

Curious first, brave second. She asks before she acts. When she gets something
wrong she says so and tries again — this is deliberate: preschool audiences
learn persistence from seeing a competent character fail safely.

### IP safety check

- [x] Two long braids with ribbons → no bob, no fringe
- [x] Star lantern, never a backpack, never a talking map
- [x] Teal/white/red palette → not pink-and-orange
- [x] No animal companion in matching boots
- [x] Stylised CGI proportions, explicitly non-photoreal
- [x] Name "Tara" is a common given word (means "star") — not a protected mark

## 2. CHIKOO — the companion

**An Indian palm squirrel. Small, greedy, loyal, wordless.**

Deliberately **not** a monkey. The other show's sidekick is a monkey in boots;
any monkey companion invites the comparison instantly. The Indian palm squirrel
is instead a creature every Indian child sees daily on a compound wall, and it
carries a lovely piece of built-in folklore — the three pale stripes down its
back.

### Flow Characters tab — paste this exactly

> Three pale cream stripes running down the back from head to tail. Small
> grey-brown squirrel with a thin, very bushy upright tail almost as long as the
> body. Tiny rounded ears, large glossy black eyes, small pink nose, cheek
> pouches that visibly bulge when full. Palm-sized — small enough to sit on a
> child's shoulder. Quick, twitchy, alert movements. 3D CGI animated style,
> DreamWorks and Pixar theatrical animated film quality. Real fur texture,
> physical weight, personality-driven expressions. NOT photorealistic. NOT flat
> 2D. IMPORTANT: three cream stripes along the back are always visible. Slim
> build with a slender tail — NOT a fat grey park squirrel, NOT a chipmunk with
> facial stripes.

### Why this order

Leading with **three cream stripes** is the whole trick. "Squirrel" alone pulls
Flow toward the North American grey squirrel or toward a well-known prehistoric
acorn-chasing character. The stripe detail is specific enough to override both,
and it happens to be the exact feature that makes the animal Indian.

### Visual signature

| Signature | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **THE TAIL QUESTION** | Chikoo is confused or doesn't know | His bushy tail curls over into a clear question-mark hook and holds for a beat |

This gives the show a second, wordless way to pose a question to the viewer —
useful for the youngest end of the audience who cannot yet follow speech.

### Personality

Never speaks. Communicates in chitters, tail shapes and pointing. Motivated
almost entirely by snacks, which is a reliable comedy engine and gives episodes
an easy secondary objective. He is the one who gets scared first, so Tara can be
brave *for* someone — much stronger than being brave alone.

### IP safety check

- [x] Palm squirrel, not a monkey → no sidekick collision
- [x] Three back stripes → not a chipmunk (facial stripes), not a grey squirrel
- [x] No boots, no clothing, no anthropomorphic standing posture
- [x] Wordless → no catchphrase to collide with anything

## 3. KAAKI — the taker

**An Indian house crow. Mischievous, comic, never frightening.**

Every show of this format needs someone who takes things. Ours is a crow —
because Indian house crows genuinely do steal shiny objects, so it is authentic
rather than borrowed, and because a bird is visually as far from a fox as it is
possible to get.

### Flow Characters tab — paste this exactly

> A grey collar of feathers around the neck and upper chest, against a glossy
> black head, wings and tail. Plump rounded body with a head slightly too large
> for it, giving a comic look. Big round amber eyes with tiny pupils. Short
> stout grey-black beak. Three small grey feathers on top of the head that lie
> flat most of the time. Hops rather than walks. Cheerful, cheeky, mischievous
> expression — playful and comic, never menacing, never scary, never sinister.
> Bright friendly daytime lighting. 3D CGI animated style, DreamWorks and Pixar
> theatrical animated film quality. Real feather texture, physical weight,
> personality-driven expressions. NOT photorealistic. NOT flat 2D. IMPORTANT:
> grey neck collar against black body is always visible. Rounded and comic —
> NOT a sleek black raven, NOT gothic, NOT a horror crow.

### Why this order

"Crow" is a heavily loaded word — Flow's associations run straight to gothic,
menacing, horror, Halloween. Two counters are built in: the **grey collar** leads
(a house crow is visibly two-tone, unlike a raven), and *four separate* explicit
non-menacing cues appear before the style block. For a preschool show, a
frightening antagonist is a channel-killer.

### Visual signature — and the interactive mechanic

| Signature | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **THE FEATHER TELL** | Kaaki is about to take something | The three grey head-feathers stand straight up and quiver |

**THE FEATHER TELL is the show's second core invention.** It gives the child
watching a reliable early-warning cue they can spot *before* the adults in the
room do. Preschoolers love knowing something the character doesn't — it converts
passive watching into leaning forward and shouting at the screen.

**The stopping mechanic — CLAP THREE TIMES**

When the feathers go up, Tara turns to camera, lifts the lantern, and asks the
viewer to **clap three times**. On the third clap Kaaki startles, drops the item,
the head-feathers flatten, and he flaps off with a comic squawk.

This is deliberately **physical, not verbal**. A spoken command would echo the
other show's catchphrase; clapping does not, and it works better for the
under-threes who cannot reliably produce a phrase but can absolutely clap. It
also gives parents a visible sign the show is landing.

Kaaki is never punished, never hurt, never chased away in anger. He usually comes
back at the end and is given something freely — the arc is *he takes because
nobody offers*, which is the same emotional engine as Grimble's, reused honestly.

### IP safety check

- [x] Bird, not a fox → no antagonist collision
- [x] Grey collar → not a raven, not any known corvid character
- [x] Clap mechanic → no verbal catchphrase to collide with
- [x] Comic and non-menacing → safe for the age band

## 4. AMMAMMA — the quest-giver

**Tara's grandmother. Warm, capable, sets the journey.**

She replaces the talking-map device. The other show uses a sentient map that
announces three locations; ours uses a grandmother who tells her granddaughter
where to go, and a lantern that tracks progress. Same structural function,
entirely different expression — and considerably warmer.

### Flow Characters tab — paste this exactly

> Silver-grey hair pulled back into a low neat bun with a small white flower
> tucked in at the side. Round wire-rimmed spectacles. Wears a soft cotton sari
> in muted sage green with a thin cream border, draped simply. Warm brown skin
> with deep laugh lines around the eyes and mouth. Kind, alert, amused
> expression. Stands and moves easily and upright — active and capable, NOT
> frail, NOT bent, NOT sad. An older woman, a grandmother. 3D CGI animated
> style, DreamWorks and Pixar theatrical animated film quality. Physical weight,
> fabric texture, personality-driven expressions. NOT photorealistic. NOT flat
> 2D.

### Note on the "NOT frail" instruction

Flow strongly associates "old woman" with frailty, stooping and sadness. Three
explicit negatives are needed to hold her upright and cheerful. This matters:
she is a competent adult who sends a child off confidently, not someone the
child is worrying about.

### Personality

She never solves the problem. She gives the three stops and a piece of practical
knowledge, then steps back. She is where the Telugu or Hindi word of the episode
comes from — taught naturally, in context, because a grandmother teaching a word
to a grandchild is simply what happens.

## 5. THE STAR LANTERN — the structural device

Not a character. Deliberately does not speak, does not have a face, and is never
animated as a creature — that is precisely what keeps it clear of the talking-map
and talking-backpack comparison.

> A small brass lantern in the shape of a five-pointed star, roughly the size of
> a child's two hands. Aged warm brass with visible hammer marks. A small ring
> handle at the top. Each of the five points contains a tiny separate light that
> can glow warm gold independently. Unlit points are dark brass. 3D CGI animated
> style, DreamWorks and Pixar theatrical quality. NOT photorealistic.

**How it drives episode structure:** five points, but only **three** light in a
standard episode — one per stop of the journey. The two remaining dark points
are the series' long game: they light only in special or seasonal episodes,
which gives returning viewers something to notice and ask about.

## COLOUR LANGUAGE

Mirrors the discipline already working for Scout and Grimble.

| Colour | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Marigold yellow** | Tara's world — safe, curious, home |
| **Deep indigo** | The unknown — before discovery, night, the far side of a journey |
| **Warm gold** | The lantern, knowledge, "you got it right" |
| **Slate grey** | Kaaki's mischief — clouds in, then clears |
| **Sunrise pink-gold** | Full resolution — the episode has landed |
| **Teal-green** | Tara herself — her kurta, her signature presence in frame |

## SAFETY POSITION — read before writing any prompt

These are **stylised 3D CGI animated characters**, not depictions of real
children. That distinction is doing real work and every prompt must protect it:

1. Every character description above ends with an explicit **NOT photorealistic
   / NOT a real child / NOT live action** clause. Never remove it.
2. Never use the words *realistic*, *photoreal*, *real child*, *live action*,
   *photograph* or *8K photo* anywhere in a Tara prompt.
3. Never upload a real child's photograph as a Flow character reference.
4. If a generated shot comes back looking photographic rather than animated,
   **discard it** — do not use it and do not try to push it further.

Per `FLOW_COMPLIANCE.md` §2, realistic synthetic humans are an absolute
prohibition. Stylised animation is the compliant path to the same creative goal,
and it is also simply what every successful preschool CGI show actually uses.

## WHAT NEVER APPEARS IN A PROMPT

| Never write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Any named character from another show | The visual description blocks above |
| "girl explorer" | "child with two braids and a star lantern" |
| "like Dora" / any show name | Nothing — describe visually, always |
| "realistic child" / "real kid" | "stylised 3D CGI animated child" |
| "backpack" | "star lantern" |
| A real place name in a peril scene | An original location name |
| Real brands, logos, packaging | Plain or original-design objects |
